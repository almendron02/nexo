# Nexo lesson pattern

Lesson 4.3 is the benchmark for the remaining Foundations lessons. New lessons should reuse its authored progression and shared block renderer while teaching their own concept on its own terms.

## The continuous lesson argument

A lesson should feel like one developing explanation rather than a stack of exercises. Use this sequence as a content checklist, not as visible chapter labels:

1. **Name the problem.** Explain what the learner is trying to express and why an English-first assumption may fail.
2. **Retrieve a first distinction.** Ask for a small prediction using only the explanation already given.
3. **Build the mental model.** Define the concept's jobs, contrasts, and limits in plain language with meaningful Spanish examples.
4. **Separate meaning from form.** First decide what the sentence means; then choose the exact form required by the subject and context.
5. **Correct the tempting shortcut.** Include a counterexample that prevents a misleading rule from becoming the learner's model.
6. **Practice with support.** Move through prediction, sorting, and construction while feedback stays beside the action and explains why.
7. **Read the concept in context.** Use connected Spanish rather than isolated example sentences. Let the learner listen before revealing the translation.
8. **Synthesize.** State how the examples work together before support disappears.
9. **Recall without choices.** Require typed retrieval of the key distinctions and forms.
10. **Produce original Spanish.** Ask the learner to communicate something true or plausible about their own world.
11. **Retrieve once more.** End with a short mixed check and a compact summary of the decision process.

## Authoring standard

- Explanation earns the interaction that follows it. Do not test a distinction before teaching it.
- Teach the sentence's meaning before its morphology.
- Use general Latin American Spanish for production.
- Important Spanish should be playable at natural and slow speeds.
- Feedback must identify the sentence's job and the evidence for the answer; avoid bare right or wrong messages.
- Support should fade from visible contrasts, to guided manipulation, to unsupported recall, to original production.
- Include at least one boundary case or counterexample when a common shortcut would produce the wrong model.
- Keep related explanation and examples in the narrow reading column. Use a wider interactive surface only when the task benefits from it.
- End with a lesson-specific completion statement that names what the learner can now do.

## Shared block roles

| Block | Best use |
| --- | --- |
| `prose` | Explanation, counterexamples, decision routines, and plain structured points |
| `comparison` | One precise contrast with parallel examples |
| `choice` | Prediction followed by immediate explanatory feedback |
| `sort` | Categorizing meanings after the categories are understood |
| `builder` | Moving from intended meaning to sentence order |
| `reading` | Connected input, audio, optional translation, and comprehension |
| `fill` | Unsupported or lightly cued form retrieval |
| `free-write` | Original production with a bounded correction path |
| `summary` | A compact decision reference, not a new explanation |

## Lesson 4.3 benchmark checks

Lesson 4.3 establishes the minimum quality bar by:

- teaching identity, classification, origin, state, and location before extended practice
- separating the verb-family decision from subject agreement and conjugation
- rejecting the permanent-versus-temporary shortcut with stable-location and short-lived-role counterexamples
- teaching the event-location boundary explicitly
- moving from prediction to guided manipulation, contextual reading, unsupported recall, and original writing
- providing a lesson-specific goal, duration, feedback path, summary, and completion state

Start Here, Modules 1–3, Lessons 4.1–4.5, and the Stage I checkpoint now use this shared system. The pre-Module-4 lessons are authored through a typed lesson factory that preserves lesson-specific explanations and exercises while keeping the renderer contract consistent. Accounts, database migration, payments, and deployment remain out of scope until these learning experiences are validated.
